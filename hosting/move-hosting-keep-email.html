Move a website and keep its domain email working

Decide whether the move needs a web-record edit or a nameserver change, then verify mail delivery independently of the website.

Lilik HartantiPublished 27 November 2026

A design studio moves its portfolio. The new homepage looks right, but clients later report bounced replies. The website and incoming mail share a domain name while relying on different DNS records. A web-only move can leave mail alone if you first identify the dependencies.

Choose the smallest change the new host supports

Open the new host’s custom-domain instructions. If you can leave the authoritative nameservers in place, changing only the relevant web records usually makes the move easier to reverse. Keep the registration and mail service active. Inventory the current apex, www and mail-related records before editing the active zone.

Check what the MX records point to. If an MX points to the bare domain and you move that domain’s address to a static web host, mail can follow the web change even though the MX text itself stayed identical. Ask the mail provider for its supported mail hostname before you proceed.

Prepare a comparison sheet for a nameserver move

Rows to compare between the active and proposed zone
RecordWhat to copy or verify
MXEvery mail exchanger and its priority. Keep the destination hostname complete.
Mail host A or AAAAThe address of a mail hostname you operate when an MX points at it.
TXT or CNAMESender authentication records from the mail provider, including each active DKIM selector, SPF and DMARC where configured.
Other service recordsVerification values, subdomains and any service-specific records your inventory identifies.

An automatic import can miss an uncommon selector name. Compare the existing zone export with each service’s instructions. Preserve separate records at their original names and avoid combining unrelated TXT values. A DNSSEC-signed domain also needs its registrar delegation and signing change coordinated with the DNS providers before you switch.

Test three independent paths

Visit a known page on the new deployment. Send an ordinary test message from an unrelated mailbox to the domain and confirm it arrives. Reply from the domain mailbox and check that the reply reaches the external account. If your mail provider offers a signing check, verify it separately. One successful inbound message says little about how other providers evaluate outgoing mail.

Keep timestamps, sender and recipient domains, and any delivery error code. Remove personal addresses and message bodies from a public support request. Delay closing the previous DNS account until the new zone serves the required records and the transition window has been accounted for.
